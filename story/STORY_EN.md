## Core idea

> **Lagunite is proven in production with humans. With AI it doesn't win yet — and we measure that in public.**

Honesty is not a disclaimer, it's the differentiator. Most frameworks claim “AI-ready” without numbers. We show where we stand.

---

## The story in three acts

### 1. Origin — proven in production
Lagunite started as the framework we used to build many websites quickly and at scale. Class names that read like what they do, shared tokens, layered components (atoms → organisms) and a real night mode. It worked: we shipped fast and maintained without pain.

### 2. Today — what we haven't solved yet
In the AI era we tried to adapt it so agents could build with Lagunite: skills, a validator, a class catalog. It is not yet more efficient than Tailwind or plain CSS in tokens spent per result. The reason is simple: models already know Tailwind and CSS by heart; they have to learn Lagunite on every task.

### 3. What we're doing — keep going, with evidence
We keep working: building real examples in the Lab, strengthening the framework, and publicly measuring what it costs to build the same screen with Lagunite, Tailwind and CSS. When Lagunite wins, the numbers will show it. Meanwhile it remains a great choice for teams who write and review their own markup.

## Copy rules

**Say:** “proven in production”, “readable classes”, “not yet”, “work in progress”, “we measure it”, concrete numbers with a link.

**Don't say (until the benchmark backs it):** “AI-first”, “AI-ready”, “built for agents” as a present claim; “the design system AI can't break”; “moat vs Tailwind”; any unmeasured comparison; dated promises about the JSON → HTML compiler.

**Tone:** first person plural, no hype, no superlatives. Tailwind is our baseline, not the enemy.

## FAQ

**Can I build with Lagunite using AI?**  
Yes, but today it costs more tokens than Tailwind or plain CSS. We publish that in the benchmark and work to close the gap.

**So why use it?**  
If your team writes and reviews its markup, Lagunite gives readable classes, tokens and design judgment out of the box. That's what made it useful in the first place.

**Is it another Tailwind?**  
Same kind of utility-first CSS, with descriptive names and a layered design system. Tailwind is our comparison baseline.

**What about the JSON → HTML compiler?**  
A paused future exploration. Not part of the product.
